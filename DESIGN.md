# Design System: Sensorr

`libs/theme/src/lib/theme/` is the source of truth for every token above.
`tools/docs/check-design.mjs` fails when this file and that folder disagree; run it
after touching either.

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "Your Friendly Digital Video Recorder"**

Not a slogan invented for this document. It is the line the product already says about
itself, in seven places: `apps/web/src/index.html:31`, `apps/web/src/manifest.json:4`,
`apps/web/src/layout/Loading/Loading.tsx:12`, `apps/web/src/pages/Login/Login.tsx:49`,
`apps/web/src/pages/Settings/Settings.tsx:78`, `apps/cli/src/main.js:42`, and — spelled
out in full — `apps/web/src/pages/KeepInTouch/KeepInTouch.tsx:155`: *"A Friendly Digital
Video Recorder. Think VCR but in modern times."* Every screen answers to that sentence.

A VCR is a machine you program once and then trust. Sensorr's subject is the **movie and
its release**: a policy sorts candidate releases into `require`, `prefer` and `avoid`
groups (`libs/sensorr/src/lib/policy.ts:79-105`), and the interface's real job is to show
what that policy did — which axis held, which one broke, what a swap would cost. The
Proposal screen is where the product is most itself: five axes (`resolution`, `source`,
`encoding`, `dub`, `language`) rendered as an old value tucked under the new one, green when the policy
still holds, red when it breaks
(`apps/web/src/components/Sensorr/Proposal.tsx:38-118`). `Followed`,
`KeepInTouch`, `Person` and `Calendar` are discovery paths — the part Radarr does not
have — but they feed the wishlist; they are not the subject.

So the surface is dense and instrumented rather than spacious. A poster declares five badge
slots — `state`, `proposal`, `focus`, `reviews`, `guests`
(`libs/ui/src/elements/Entity/Poster/Poster.tsx:22-28`) — plus a credits strip. A row of metadata is monospaced because it is data, not prose. The chrome is
near-black so the posters carry the color, and the one saturated green is reserved for the
thing the machine decided. Emoji do the work of an icon set: there is no icon font and no
icon package in `libs/ui`, `⌛`, `📹`, `🚨`, `🔒`, `📭` and their neighbours are the
vocabulary.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Instrumented: every state a job can be in has a badge, and every badge says which.
- Discreet: chrome recedes to near-black, the artwork is the color.
- Dense: information per screen is high on purpose; a library is a wall of posters.
- Detailed: five-axis diffs, policy ranks, byte-exact sizes.
- Fine: 1px borders, 0.25em radii, no ornament above the data.
- UX-friendly: every control says its state before you touch it.

## Colors

One saturated green against a near-black chrome, plus a full semantic set that is used for
meaning and never for decoration.

### Primary
- **Vivid Spring Green** (`primary`): the one saturated color in the system. It marks what
  the machine decided or what the user is standing on: the active navigation item, a
  held policy axis, a wishlisted tag, a primary button. It comes from a blend of TMDB's
  historical green and an Amazon redesign concept seen on Dribbble — a lineage that
  explains its saturation, which is far above anything else in the palette. 87 references
  across 59 files (83 quoted `'primary'`, 4 `theme.colors.primary`).
- **Spring Green Dark / Darker / Darkest** (`primaryDark`, `primaryDarker`,
  `primaryDarkest`): the hover, active and disabled steps of anything green. Saturation
  drops one point per step (99 → 98 → 97 → 96) while lightness drops three, so pressing a
  button reads as the same color going quiet rather than a different color.
- **Spring Green Lightest** (`primaryLightest`): a near-white green tint, reserved for
  type set on a green field.

### Secondary
- **Forest Accent** (`accent`, `accentDark`, `accentDarker`, `accentDarkest`): the same
  hue at 85–95% saturation, four steps darker. Its job is the *second* green on a screen
  that already has a primary one — a link hovering away from `primary`
  (`libs/theme/src/lib/theme/variants.ts:20-25`), a secondary button, the `darken`
  backdrop.
- **Deep Petrol** (`secondary`, `secondaryLight`): the only cool hue in the palette
  (197°, 9% and 19% lightness). Used as a ground behind bright content.

### Tertiary
- **Plex Amber** (`plex`): not a Sensorr color. It is Plex's brand orange, present so that
  a Plex-owned affordance looks like Plex and not like Sensorr.

### Neutral
- **Reading Grey** (`text`, `textLight`, `textLighter`, `textLightest`): the type ramp.
  In the default dark mode these run *up* from 90% to 98% lightness, because `text` is the
  quiet body value and the `Light` suffix means "closer to the surface it contrasts with".
- **Stacked Surfaces** (`white`, `grayLightest` → `grayDarkest`): the layer stack.
  `white` is the page, `grayLightest`/`grayLighter`/`grayLight` are the panels that sit on
  it, `gray` is a filled chip, `grayDark`/`grayDarker`/`grayDarkest` are borders in their
  three states. The names describe the *light-mode* appearance; in dark mode every one of
  them resolves to a near-black (`libs/theme/src/lib/theme/colors.ts:116-127`).
- **Absolutes** (`whitePure`, `blackPure`): the only two colors that never invert. Use
  them when you need actual white or actual black regardless of mode — type on a green
  field, a knob on a photograph.

### Semantic
- **Signal Red** (`error`, `errorDark`, `errorDarker`, `errorDarkest`): a broken policy, a
  destructive button, a failed job.
- **Signal Amber** (`warning`): a job that ran but did not finish its work.
- **Signal Blue** (`info`): a neutral notice.
- **Signal Green** (`success`): the same value as `primary`. They are one color with two
  names, which is why a successful job and a primary action look identical, and why green
  is never available as a decorative choice.

### Named Rules

**The Poster Paints the Screen Rule.** Sensorr does not pick accent colors for content —
it takes them from the artwork at runtime. `libs/palette/` loads the poster into a canvas,
ships the pixels to a Web Worker, and gets back five values:
`backgroundColor`, `color`, `alternativeColor`, `accentColor` and `colorfulColor`
(`libs/palette/src/lib/palette.worker.js:177-206`). The worker pairs the dominant color
with every other extracted color, keeps only pairs above a contrast threshold, and
**lightens or darkens the partner until the pair reaches 4.5:1**
(`libs/palette/src/lib/palette.worker.js:136-158`) — so a palette taken from a poster is
WCAG-corrected before it is ever painted. `usePalette` caches the result in
`sessionStorage` keyed by color mode plus image URL
(`libs/palette/src/lib/usePalette.js:24-40`). Six modules import `@sensorr/palette`:
`Picture`, `Billboard`, `Shadow`, `Pretty`, `Poster` and the Details page; `Badge` and the
Details `Actions` take a palette as a prop without importing the type. **When a surface
shows one movie, the theme's neutrals are the frame and the poster's palette is the
content.** Do not hardcode an accent for a movie-scoped surface.

**The One Green Rule.** `primary` and `success` are the same `hsla(154, 99%, 41%, 1)`.
Green therefore always means *the system says yes*: this is on, this is kept, this held.
Never reach for green because a surface needs a color.

**The Declared-and-Unreachable Light Mode Rule.** `libs/theme/src/lib/theme/colors.ts:72-98`
defines a complete 27-line `light` palette, and `colors.ts:141` registers it as
`modes.light`. `libs/theme/src/lib/theme/index.ts:11-14` sets
`initialColorModeName: 'dark'` and `useColorSchemeMediaQuery: false`, and **nothing in the
application calls `setColorMode` or `useColorMode`** — the only references to `colorMode`
anywhere in `apps/` and `libs/` are reads, in
`apps/web/src/pages/Details/components/Actions.tsx:66` and
`libs/palette/src/lib/usePalette.js:6`. The light palette is therefore dead code that
stays correct: it is documented debt, not a feature. Keep it consistent when you add a
color — add the token to `raw` and both mode objects — but do not describe Sensorr as
having a light theme, and do not build a toggle without deciding first whether the light
palette is still wanted.

## Typography

**Display Font:** Raleway (falling back through Frutiger, Calibri, Gill Sans, Tahoma,
Helvetica Neue, Arial, sans-serif)
**Body Font:** Open Sans (falling back through Segoe UI, Candara, DejaVu Sans, Trebuchet
MS, Verdana, sans-serif)
**Label/Mono Font:** Fira Code (falling back through Consolas, Andale Mono, DejaVu Sans
Mono, Monaco, Courier New, monospace)

Each stack ends in an emoji run — `Apple Color Emoji`, `Segoe UI Emoji`, `Noto Color
Emoji`, `Twemoji Mozilla` and friends — before its generic family
(`libs/theme/src/lib/theme/font.ts:2-7`). That is not padding: emoji *are* the icon set,
so they have to resolve on every platform. A `-no-emoji` twin of each stack exists for the
rare place that must not fall through to an emoji face.

**Character:** Raleway's geometric caps make headings read as labels on a machine rather
than as editorial titles. Open Sans keeps long English strings — overviews, job logs —
readable at small sizes. Fira Code carries everything that is *data*: release names,
resolutions, byte counts, policy ranks. The three faces map cleanly onto three jobs, and
mixing them is how the interface tells you what kind of thing you are looking at.

### Hierarchy
- **h1** (800, `2em`, 1.5): page-level title. One per screen.
- **h2** (800, `1.75em`, 1.5): section heading.
- **h3** (800, `1.5em`, 1.5): sub-section heading.
- **h4** (800, `1.25em`, 1.5) / **h5** (800, `1em`, 1.5) / **h6** (800, `0.875em`, 1.5):
  the remaining steps. `libs/theme/src/lib/theme/global.ts:32-55` wires `h1`…`h6` to
  `heading.default` and to font-size steps 0…5, so the heading level *is* the size — there
  is no free-floating display size.
- **label** (800, `1em`, 1.2): a field's name, in the heading face
  (`libs/ui/src/inputs/Label/Label.tsx:15-23`). Headings and labels share a face on
  purpose: a field label is a small heading.
- **body** (400, `1em`, 1.2): the default. Set on `body` by
  `libs/theme/src/lib/theme/global.ts:5-13`.
- **body-long** (400, `1em`, 1.5): the same face at reading line-height, for anything
  longer than a line — overviews, `Warning` subtitles.
- **field** (400, `0.875em`, 1.5): text typed into an input or a select, one size below
  body so a form reads as denser than prose.
- **button** (600, `0.875em`, 1): control text. Semibold, one size below body, line-height
  reset to 1 so a button's height comes from its padding
  (`libs/theme/src/lib/theme/variants.ts:28-53`).
- **caption** (600, `0.625em`, 1.2): a poster's year and genre line
  (`libs/ui/src/elements/Entity/Poster/Poster.tsx:398-415`).
- **mono** (400, `0.875em`, 1): badge labels. Transition pills set it at `0.75em`, the same
  size in the active card and in the list.
- **mono-strong** (600, `0.75em`, 1.2): a tag.

### Named Rules

**The Monospace-Means-Machine Rule.** Fira Code is never a stylistic choice. It marks a
value the machine produced or parsed: a release name, an axis value, a resolution, a size,
a count, a policy rank. If a human wrote the string, it is Open Sans. If a parser produced
it, it is Fira Code.

**The No Free Sizes Rule.** Every font size is an index into one thirteen-step scale
(`libs/theme/src/lib/theme/sizes.ts:1-15`), and that same scale is the spacing scale.
`fontSize: 5` and `paddingY: 5` are the same `0.875em`. Never write a font size as a
literal; take the nearest step.

## Layout

**Two breakpoints, three bands.** `libs/theme/src/lib/theme/breakpoints.ts` declares
`768px` and `1200px`. Because theme-ui reads style arrays against that list, almost every
responsive rule in the codebase is a two-entry array — mobile value, then everything from
768px up — and a third entry is used only where the desktop band genuinely differs (the
header's secondary items, `apps/web/src/layout/Header/Header.tsx:96-99`).

**One scale for space and type.** `sizes` is thirteen `em` steps and the theme registers
it as both `space` and `fontSizes` (`libs/theme/src/lib/theme/index.ts:16-21`). It runs
**descending**: index `0` is the largest (`2em`) and index `12` is zero. So a higher index
means *less*: `paddingX: 4` is `1em`, `paddingY: 8` is `0.5em`, `margin: 12` is the way
this codebase writes `margin: 0`. Everything is in `em`, so a container's `fontSize` scales
its whole subtree — which is how a `Poster` becomes a thumbnail inside a `Card` by nothing
more than `fontSize: 8` (`libs/ui/src/elements/Entity/Card/Card.tsx:46-49`).

**Two list models.** A wall of posters is a virtualized responsive grid whose cell size
switches at the first breakpoint — 120×225 on mobile, 204×346 above
(`libs/ui/src/elements/Grid/Grid.tsx:63-66`) — scrolling inside `#body`, not the window.
A list of rows is a `Card` at a fixed `7.5em` height and `35em` max width. Filter options
lay out as `repeat(auto-fill, 22em)` (`libs/ui/src/inputs/Options/Options.tsx:29-33`).

**Two navigations for two shells.** Installed as a PWA, navigation is a bottom bar with
icon-over-label items and `padding-bottom: max(0.75em, env(safe-area-inset-bottom))`. In a
browser it is a horizontal scroller at the top, with a second row of sub-routes that
appears only inside its branch (`apps/web/src/layout/Header/elements/Navigation.tsx:40-94`).

## Elevation & Depth

**Flat, with tonal stacking.** There is no elevation system. `libs/theme` exports a
`shadows` object with two entries (`libs/theme/src/lib/theme/colors.ts:144-147`), the theme
registers it (`index.ts:24`), and **nothing else in `apps/` or `libs/` references
`shadows`** — zero consumers, verified by grep. Across the whole of `libs/ui` there are 8
`boxShadow` declarations: 3 are `boxShadow: 'none'` resetting `react-select`, 4 are the
`Range` thumb's focus ring, and 1 is the Poster glow described below.

Depth comes from tone instead. Surfaces step through `white` → `grayLightest` →
`grayLighter` → `grayLight` → `gray`, which in dark mode is 0% → 2% → 4% → 7% → 10%
lightness — a two-to-three point step per layer. A panel is separated from its page by
being a shade less black, and by a 1px `grayDark` border, not by a shadow. Where a surface
must overlap another it uses `zIndex` and a transform, never a cast shadow (`Pane` slides
in on `translate3d`, `libs/ui/src/atoms/Pane/Pane.tsx:46-63`).

### Shadow Vocabulary
- **Poster glow** (`box-shadow: 0px 3px 30px <poster colorfulColor>`): the single
  intentional shadow in the system, at
  `libs/ui/src/elements/Entity/Poster/Poster.tsx:483`. It is not a drop shadow — it is a
  30px blur with no spread, tinted with the *poster's own* most chromatic color, falling
  back to `primary`. It reads as a lamp behind the artwork.
- **Focus ring** (`box-shadow: 0px 0px 0px 2px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.1)`, growing to `8px` on
  hover and `14px` on drag): the `Range` thumb only
  (`libs/ui/src/inputs/Range/Range.tsx:183-192`). A spread-only halo, zero blur — a ring,
  not a shadow.

### Named Rules

**The No Shadow Rule.** A new surface gets a tonal step and a 1px border. If you find
yourself reaching for `box-shadow` to separate two things, the two things need different
`gray*` values instead. The only sanctioned exceptions are a color taken from artwork
(the poster glow) and a spread-only focus halo.

## Shapes

Radius is assigned by **role**, not by a size scale, and the roles are visible in the
tally across `apps/` and `libs/`: `0.25em` appears 28 times, `50%` 14 times, `2em` 7
times, `0.25rem` 6 times, `1.5em` twice.

- **Controls and containers** take `0.25em` (`rounded.control`) — buttons, code tags,
  panels. Just enough to not be a raw rectangle.
- **Text fields and selects** take `0.25rem` (`rounded.field`)
  (`libs/theme/src/lib/theme/variants.ts:82` and `:126`). The unit difference from
  `control` is real: a field's corner does not grow with its font size, a button's does.
- **Badges and tags** are fully round: `2em` on a `2em`-tall badge, `1.5em` on a tag,
  `50%` when a badge has an emoji and no label
  (`libs/ui/src/atoms/Badge/Badge.tsx:52-79`). A pill means *a state*; a rectangle means
  *an action*. Transition pills and the job filters of `/jobs` take `1em`.
- **Avatars and status dots** are circles, ringed with a `0.25em` solid `grayLightest`
  border so they read as stickers on top of a poster
  (`libs/ui/src/elements/Entity/Poster/Poster.tsx:352-365`).

Borders are the system's main drawing tool and they are almost always `1px solid`, colored
`grayDark` at rest. The only registered border token is `borders.avatar`
(`solid 0.375em white`, `libs/theme/src/lib/theme/borders.ts`).

The theme's `radii` array holds a single `5em` entry and **no component references it**
(verified by grep: `radii` appears only in `sizes.ts` and `index.ts`). Treat the four
`rounded` tokens above as the real vocabulary. The `50%` of a circular badge is not among
them because the format only accepts `px`, `rem` and `em`; it lives in this section and in
`.impeccable/design.json` instead.

### Named Rules

**The Pill-Is-A-State Rule.** Anything fully rounded is read-only information about state:
a wishlist badge, a policy axis, a tag. Anything with a `0.25em` corner is something you
can press. Do not round an action into a pill.

## Components

### Buttons
- **Shape:** softly cornered (`0.25em`), with a `0.125em` border that is always drawn,
  even on the filled variant (`libs/theme/src/lib/theme/variants.ts:43-52`). The border is
  what makes the filled and outline variants the same size.
- **Primary (contain):** `primary` fill, pure white text, `0.75em 1em` padding, semibold
  `0.875em` body text. Hover and active step the fill to `primaryDark` then
  `primaryDarker`; disabled goes to `primaryDarkest` with `hsl(0, 0%, 80%)` text
  (`libs/ui/src/atoms/Button/Button.tsx:21-38`).
- **Hover / Focus:** `color 200ms ease-in-out, background-color 200ms ease-in-out,
  border-color 200ms ease-in-out`. All three properties move together, which is why a
  button never appears to change shape.
- **Outline:** transparent fill, the color on the border *and* the text, moving to the
  `Dark` / `Darker` / `Darkest` step for hover / active / disabled. Seven colors are
  available to either variant: `primary`, `primaryDark`, `accent`, `error`, `black`,
  `white`, `gray`.
- **Reset:** `button.reset` strips appearance, border, background and font weight and is
  the base for every icon button in the app
  (`libs/theme/src/lib/theme/variants.ts:29-42`).

### Chips
Two distinct things, and they do not share a shape.
- **Badge** (`libs/ui/src/atoms/Badge/Badge.tsx`): a `2em`-tall pill, `gray` fill,
  monospaced capitalized label, and a `50%` circle when it carries only an emoji. It is
  the carrier for movie and person state, and for `Focus` metrics (rating, runtime,
  popularity, vote count). Pass a `palette` and it repaints itself in the poster's colors:
  `backgroundColor: palette.color`, `color: palette.backgroundColor`.
- **Tag** (`libs/ui/src/elements/Entity/Tag/Tag.tsx`): a `1.5em`-radius `primary` pill
  with `white` text — which in dark mode resolves to *black* type on green, since `white`
  is one of the inverting surface tokens. It is a link, not a status.

### Cards / Containers
- **Corner Style:** square. `Card` sets no radius; its child `Poster` carries the artwork's
  own edges.
- **Background:** transparent over the page. Separation comes from height (`7.5em`) and
  `contain: strict`.
- **Shadow Strategy:** none. See Elevation & Depth.
- **Border:** none on the card itself; 1px `gray` dividers between rows.
- **Internal Padding:** `1em` (step 4) between poster and text, `1em` before the trailing
  action.

### Inputs / Fields
- **Style:** transparent background, `1px solid grayDark` border, `0.25rem` radius,
  `0.5em 1em` padding, `text` color, full width, native outline suppressed.
- **Focus:** the border goes to `grayDarkest` and nothing else changes.
- **Disabled:** border falls back to `gray` and the text to `grayDarkest`
  (`libs/theme/src/lib/theme/variants.ts:134-137`).
- **Error:** no dedicated variant exists. An invalid field is currently reported outside
  the field, not on it.

### Navigation
- **Browser:** a horizontal scroller of semibold `0.875em` body text at `opacity: 0.33`,
  rising to `0.66` on hover above the first breakpoint and to `1` when active — the active
  item is *not* colored, it is simply the only one at full opacity
  (`apps/web/src/layout/Header/elements/Navigation.tsx:136-183`). A chevron introduces the
  sub-route row, which only exists inside its branch.
- **PWA:** a bottom bar of inline-SVG icons over `0.625em` labels, `grayLighter` ground,
  1px `gray` top border, `grayDark` at rest and `primary` when active. Items ripple on
  pointer-down (`use-ripple-hook`).
- **Both:** transitions are `opacity ease 300ms` (web) and `color 200ms ease-in-out` (app).

### Transition Pill (signature)
The Proposals screen renders each policy axis as two overlapping pills: the old value
underneath, the new value on top of it, `0.75em` over its right end
(`apps/web/src/components/Sensorr/Proposal.tsx`). The shape comes from the job filters of
`/jobs` (`apps/web/src/pages/Jobs/Jobs.tsx:365-395`): `1em` radius, no border, one hue
stepped down in lightness. The new value carries the state, the old one takes a darker
tint of the same hue. Both halves are set in regular weight:
- `held` (the new value satisfies `require`): `primaryDarkest` over `accentDarkest`,
  `whitePure` over `primaryLightest`. Not `primary`: white on it measures 2.04:1, too low
  for `0.75em` type. White on `primaryDarkest` measures 3.40:1, below AA, kept for the
  7-point step that keeps the overlap visible.
- `broken` (the new value is in `avoid`, or a `require` was lost): `errorDark` over
  `errorDarkest`, a 10-point step; white on `errorDark` measures 5.15:1.
- `moved` and `quiet` (no group has an opinion on the new value): `grayDark` over `gray`.
- `same`: the new value alone, at `opacity: 0.3`.
- The card header and the list rows draw the size the same way, lightest owned release under the proposed
  one, with a forced `state`: `held` when lighter, `broken` when heavier, `quiet` below the
  "Same size below" threshold.

There is no separator: the overlap says "becomes", the hue says what the policy thinks,
and the `title` still spells `x264 ~ x265` for the tooltip. The policy marks (`*`, `!`, the
`prefer` rank) stay out of the pill: at `0.75em` they read as a stray dot, and the hue
already says what the policy thinks. **This is the component that makes Sensorr look like Sensorr; extend its
vocabulary rather than inventing a second diff widget.**

### Empty and Error States (signature)
`Warning` (`libs/ui/src/atoms/Warning/Warning.tsx`) is the one shape for "nothing here" and
"something went wrong": a `5em` emoji, an uppercase bold title, a body-line subtitle,
centered, with `2.5em` padding and `4.5em` at the bottom so it sits optically centered.
The emoji carries the meaning — `📭` no stories, `🚨` policy broken, `⌛` loading. Use it
instead of writing a bespoke empty state.

### Motion
- `opacity 400ms ease-in-out` is the house transition, 9 occurrences — anything appearing
  or leaving.
- `border-color 100ms ease-in-out` on fields: fast, because it answers a keystroke.
- `200ms ease-in-out` on buttons: color, background and border together.
- `opacity ease 300ms` on web navigation.
- `background-color 800ms ease-in-out` on `Shadow`, the slowest thing in the app, because
  it is a poster's color bleeding into the page
  (`libs/ui/src/atoms/Shadow/Shadow.tsx:15`).
- Route changes use the View Transitions API, five named transitions at
  `400ms cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1)`: `fade`, `top`, `forward`, `backward`, and `root`
  explicitly set to `animation: none`
  (`libs/theme/src/lib/theme/modules.css:27-104`).
- Keyframes live in `libs/theme/src/lib/theme/animations.ts`: `placeholder`, `bounce`,
  `pulse`, `blink`, `spin`.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** take colors from `libs/theme/src/lib/theme/colors.ts` by token name. The literal
  `hsl(0, 0%, 100%)` in `Button.tsx` and the `hsl(0, 0%, 10%)` family beside it are
  pre-existing exceptions, not a pattern to copy.
- **Do** use the shared scale for both spacing and type, remembering it runs descending —
  `12` is zero and `0` is `2em`.
- **Do** let `usePalette` color anything scoped to one movie, and keep the theme's neutrals
  as the frame around it.
- **Do** separate surfaces with a tonal step plus a 1px `grayDark` border.
- **Do** reach for `Badge` for a state, `Tag` for a link, `Button` for an action.
- **Do** put machine-produced strings in the monospace face and human-written strings in
  the body face.
- **Do** add a variant by adding a named export to the component's `*.stories.tsx` and
  checking it at `/design/:stage/:component` while `yarn web` runs.
- **Do** run `node tools/docs/check-design.mjs` after editing `libs/theme` or this file's
  frontmatter.

### Don't:
- **Don't** add a `box-shadow`. The system is flat; use a tonal step.
- **Don't** use green for anything other than "the system says yes". `primary` and
  `success` are the same value, and that identity is the meaning.
- **Don't** round an action into a pill, or square off a state badge.
- **Don't** fill or shadow an input to show focus — move `border-color` and nothing else.
- **Don't** write a font size, a padding or a margin as a raw value; take a scale step.
- **Don't** assume light mode works. The `light` palette is declared and unreachable; see
  the named rule in Colors.
- **Don't** introduce an icon library. Emoji and inline SVG are the icon set, and the font
  stacks are built around that.
- **Don't** change `primary` to fix the contrast gap below. That is a brand decision, not
  a styling one.

### Known deviation: primary buttons fail WCAG AA (recorded 2026-09-18)

Pure white on `primary` measures **2.04:1** — `hsla(154, 99%, 41%, 1)` is `#01d076`,
relative luminance 0.4642, against white's 1.0. AA asks 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for
large text; this clears neither. It affects the filled primary `Button`
(`libs/ui/src/atoms/Button/Button.tsx:21-38`) and the active tab of the
`/design` gallery (`apps/web/src/pages/Design/Design.tsx:58-61`). The `held` transition
pill left `primary` for `primaryDarkest` on 2026-09-23 for this reason. The disabled primary
button is the same gap one step down: `hsl(0, 0%, 80%)` on `primaryDarkest` measures
**2.11:1**. Both figures were computed here and match `@google/design.md@0.4.0 lint`.

Black on the same green measures 10.29:1 and would pass, but inverting the primary button's
text changes how the product reads on all 87 `primary` references. **Not fixed on purpose:**
the green is the identity and moving it is a brand decision for Thomas, not a styling fix.
Recorded here so the next reader does not rediscover it as a bug. Measured with the sRGB
relative-luminance formula, WCAG 2.1 §1.4.3.
